# Chapter 2: NetLogo and Physics

- **Author:** William M. Spears
- **PDF pages:** 58–85 (book pp. 27–53; PDF = book + 31)
- **Models:** `spring1D.nlogo`, `spring2D.nlogo`, `solar_system.nlogo` (NetLogo 4.1.2). All three are two-body systems. Their value for BPP is as **integrator and verification tests**: conservation of linear momentum, angular momentum and energy, plus the force-cap (Fmax) idea used everywhere later.

## Common machinery (Listings 2.1–2.6, PDF 64–75)

- **Synchronous update:** the loop `run-and-monitor` calls `ask turtles [ap]` and *then* `ask turtles [move]`. All forces are computed from the same snapshot before anyone moves.
- **The `ap` step**, in order:
  1. **Friction first:** `v ← (1 − FR)·v`. This is a per-step multiplicative damping; FR is the Friction slider.
  2. **Forces:** sum the pairwise forces F.
  3. **Velocity:** `dv = Δt·F/m`, then `v ← v + dv`.
  4. **Position:** `Δx = Δt·v`, then `x ← x + Δx`.

  This is **semi-implicit (symplectic) Euler**, the same family Bullet uses.
- **The `move` step** also logs `ke = m v²/2`, `lm = m v`, and (2D) the angular momentum `L = r·m·v·sin θ`. Here r is the lever arm from the centre of mass and θ is the angle between the momentum and the lever arm (Listing 2.6).
- **Centre of mass:** COM = Σ m_i x_i / Σ m_i (Eq. 2.1). It is drawn as a red patch.
- **Units** are NetLogo patches/pixels. The world is **toroidal**.

## Entries

### 2.1 One-dimensional spring, `spring1D.nlogo` (Sec. 2.1, Figs. 2.1–2.7, PDF 58–72)

**Shows:** Hooke's law oscillation, Newton's 3rd law giving zero total momentum, and the energy trade-off.

**Agents:** 2 point masses on the x-axis.
- Turtle 0 has mass 1. Turtle 1 has mass = `Mass_Ratio` (settable ≥ 1, at initialisation only).
- Initial positions: each moves `1 + random(D/2)` from the origin in opposite directions (headings 90° and 270°). The initial velocity is 0.

**Force (Listing 2.4):**
- Neighbour to the right: `F = k(r − D)`.
- Neighbour to the left: `F = k(D − r)`.
- Potential energy is `PE = k(S − D)²/2` (footnote 4).

**Default sliders (Fig. 2.1):**

| Slider | Default |
|---|---|
| Mass_Ratio | 1 |
| Spring_Constant k | 10 |
| Friction | 0.000 |
| Time_Step Δt | 0.0010 |
| Desired_Spring_Length D | 50 |

**Results to reproduce:**
1. **Symmetric oscillation:** separation oscillates about 50 between 50 + x and 50 − x (Fig. 2.2).
2. **Stiffness:** lower k (e.g., 1) gives slower, longer oscillations.
3. **Mass ratio 10:** the heavy mass barely moves and the COM sits near it (the car/wheel analogy).
4. **Damping:** friction 0.001 switched on after 5,000 time units damps the separation to D (Fig. 2.3).
5. **Larger Δt** runs faster with similar behaviour (Fig. 2.4) and shows small fluctuations in total energy.
6. **Linear momentum** stays exactly 0 at Δt = 0.001.
7. **Energy:** KE + PE is constant with friction 0 and Δt 0.001 (Fig. 2.5). KE is maximal at separation D; PE is maximal at the extremes.
8. **Friction drains energy:** with friction > 0, total energy decays to 0 (Fig. 2.6). This is heat, not a violation.
9. **Changing D or k mid-run** steps the total energy to a new constant level, because the system was "opened".
10. **Toroidal artefact:** with D = 500 (slider max raised to 500), particles cross the world edge. The separation graph jumps and the energy has two levels (Fig. 2.7).

**BPP notes:**
- Two spheres with gravity off, a virtual spring applied as a Lua force, and a live plot or HUD of S, momentum and energy. Effort: easy.
- It is the **first integrator unit test** of the BPP AP pipeline: momentum must stay 0 and energy must stay flat.
- Optional: compare against Bullet's own `btGeneric6DofSpringConstraint` spring.
- BPP has no toroidal world, so the D = 500 artefact cannot occur. Either skip it, or reproduce it deliberately with periodic boundary wrap to show why non-Euclidean space breaks the physics.
- Decide whether to reproduce NetLogo's `(1−FR)` per-step friction exactly. It is Δt-dependent; the continuous equivalent is a damping rate of −ln(1−FR)/Δt. **This matters for the plan's "friction fixes Ch. 13"** (see bottom).

### 2.2 Two-dimensional rotating spring, `spring2D.nlogo` (Sec. 2.2, Figs. 2.8–2.10, PDF 73–77)

**Shows:** a spring that spins about its COM. It demonstrates conservation of angular momentum and centrifugal stretching.

**Agents:** 2 point masses in 2D.
- The lighter (turtle 0, mass 1) gets `vx = Angular_Motion`.
- The heavier gets `vx = −Angular_Motion / Mass_Ratio`, so total momentum is 0 (Listing 2.7).

**Force (Listing 2.5):** `F = k(r − D)` along the unit vector (the Fx, Fy components).

**Default sliders (Fig. 2.9):**

| Slider | Default |
|---|---|
| Angular_Motion | 200 |
| Mass_Ratio | 10 |
| Spring_Constant | 10 |
| Friction | 0.000 |
| Time_Step | 0.0010 |
| Desired_Spring_Length | 50 |

**Results to reproduce:**
1. **Rotation:** the spring rotates about the COM while stretching and compressing.
2. **Momentum:** linear momentum (x and y) stays 0. Angular momentum stays constant up to numerical error.
3. **Sign convention:** negative L means clockwise.
4. **Centrifugal stretch:** separation can **exceed 2D = 100**, and the **mean separation is greater than D** (Fig. 2.10).
5. **Energy:** the energy cycles are more complex than a sinusoid.

**Ambiguity:** the initial geometry is not stated in the text. For an x-velocity to create spin the masses must be separated along y; Fig. 2.8 shows a roughly vertical spring. The porter must pick the initial positions (probably random along y as in 1D, rotated 90°).

**BPP notes:**
- Easy.
- Adds the angular-momentum monitor, which is reusable for formation checks in Ch. 3.
- ★ 3D: give an off-plane initial velocity. Angular momentum then fixes the rotation plane, which makes a good check that the 3D force code has no torque bias.
- ★ A real-world version: two drones holding a virtual spring while orbiting. This shows the drone controller's lag as energy drift.

### 2.3 One-planet solar system, `solar_system.nlogo` (Sec. 2.3, Figs. 2.11–2.15, PDF 78–85)

**Shows:** why a 1/r² force law breaks in discrete time near r → 0, why capping velocity fails, and why **capping the force (Fmax)** works. This is the origin of Fmax in every later AP law.

**Agents:**
- Sun: mass = `Mass_of_Sun`, at (0, 0), initial vy = −AM/M_sun.
- Planet: mass 1, at **(15, 0)**, initial vy = +AM (AM = `Angular_Motion`).
- There is no randomness, so runs are deterministic.
- The planet draws its orbit (pen down).

**Force (Listing 2.8):** `F = G·m1·m2 / r²`, attractive. |F| is capped at `Force_Maximum`. The velocity cap is `Velocity_Maximum`; its implementation is not shown, so presumably the speed magnitude is clipped. The PE formula is in ref. [240].

**Default sliders (Fig. 2.11):**

| Slider | Default |
|---|---|
| Angular_Motion | 15 |
| Mass_of_Sun | 100 |
| Gravitational_Constant G | 20 |
| Force_Maximum | 1,000,000 (effectively off) |
| Velocity_Maximum | 1000 (off) |
| Time_Step | 0.100 |

Monitors: total energy, planet velocity, force on planet, Lmx, Lmy and L.

**Experiments and results to reproduce:**

**a. Angular_Motion × sun mass, Δt = 0.1 (Fig. 2.12).**
- Runs: AM ∈ {15, 10} × M_sun ∈ {100, 95}.
- AM 15 gives a large ellipse; AM 10 gives a smaller one.
- A smaller sun mass gives a larger orbit, and the effect is bigger for the bigger orbit.
- Linear momentum stays 0; L and E stay very steady.

**b. AM ∈ {5, 0}, M_sun 100, Δt 0.1 (Fig. 2.13).**
- **AM 5:** the planet escapes. This is a discretisation error.
- **AM 0:** nonsense, and the total energy changes radically. With zero angular momentum the planet should fall through the sun and end ≤ 15 units to its left; it does not.

**c. Same with Δt = 0.001 (Fig. 2.14).**
- **AM 5:** a reasonable orbit, with momentum and energy conserved.
- **AM 0:** still wrong; the planet keeps moving left indefinitely, because the 1/r² force blows up near r = 0.

**d. Velocity cap (AM 15, M_sun 100, Δt 0.1). The planet's maximum speed without a cap is about 15.**

| Velocity_Maximum | AM | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | 15 | Runs well, with a slight linear-momentum change |
| 9 | 15 | Planet plunges into the sun and stays; momenta oscillate wildly; energy varies a lot |
| 12, 9 or 1 | 0 | Disaster: large changes in momentum and energy |

Conclusion: **velocity capping fails.**

**e. Force cap Force_Maximum = 1 (Fig. 2.15), with AM ∈ {9, 4, 1, 0}.**
- **Orbits precess** into a flower-petal / rosette shape, and the planet stays bound.
- Energy and momenta are conserved well; lowering Δt reduces the error further.
- **AM 0:** separation oscillates **0 to 15** (correct fall-through), and momenta stay 0.
- **Peak speeds:** "5.6, 5, 4 and 9 (when AM is zero, one, four and nine)" (PDF 84–85, pp. 52–53). The pairing is as printed and looks odd (AM 9 → 9 is plausible, but AM 0 → 5.6 versus AM 1 → 5 is non-monotone). Re-measure rather than trust it.

**Lesson stated for Ch. 3:** Fmax is a reasonable option, orbital precession is not a problem for formation tasks, and Fmax "can be used to our advantage, to help set parameters theoretically". This is the phase-transition formula in Ch. 3.

**BPP notes:**
- Easy for the particle version.
- Groundwork needed:
  - an **orbit trail renderer** (polyline of past positions);
  - **live plots** (energy, momenta), or CSV logging plus an external plot;
  - a deterministic initial state, so the porter can compare figure-for-figure.
- Reproducing (b)–(c) exposes BPP's fixed-step issue: Bullet's internal 1/60 s substep versus AP's Δt. The porter must decide whether AP particles use Bullet integration (forces applied as `applyCentralForce`) or a separate Lua integrator. **For the verification tests use the Lua integrator so Δt is controllable.**
- ★ **Drone version:** one drone orbits a fixed "sun" waypoint under a capped 1/r² law at 5 Hz. The velocity-cap versus force-cap comparison maps directly onto real limits: the PX4-style controller has a max speed, which is effectively a velocity cap, and per the text that **hurts**. This gives a concrete test of why the Newtonian law misbehaves on drones and links to the jitter issue and Ch. 13.
- ★ **3D:** an inclined orbit, and two planets for real precession, which the text says causes rosettes.

## Contradictions / additions to the existing plan

1. **Missing groundwork: verification tests.** The plan has none. Ch. 2 sets the precedent that every AP sim should monitor **COM drift, linear momentum, angular momentum and energy** as correctness checks; Ch. 3 reuses this. Add these three scenes (spring1D, spring2D, solar) as BPP regression tests of the AP integrator, run before porting anything else.
2. **Friction model matters for the "friction fixes Ch. 13" item.** NetLogo friction is `v ← (1−FR)v` per AP step, applied *before* the force update. In the drone port, AP runs at 5 Hz with waypoints, so "velocity" is the waypoint offset. Decide whether friction acts on the AP virtual velocity (book-faithful) or on the drone. In the book's Ch. 3 formation runs Friction = 0.5 per step at Δt = 0.5–1.0, which is very heavy damping.
3. **Velocity caps are harmful (Sec. 2.3d), which bears on the Newtonian-jitter explanation.** Real drones impose a velocity/acceleration cap, so the AP dynamics they execute are no longer Newtonian (momentum and energy are not conserved). This, together with actuator lag, is a likely contributor to the observed jitter. The text's prescribed remedy is **Fmax**: keep the AP-computed accelerations well inside what the vehicle can follow. Check the existing drone runs' Fmax relative to the controller limits.
4. ★ **Idea not in the plan:** "Hooke's law formations" (listed in the plan) should begin with spring1D/2D as their unit test.
