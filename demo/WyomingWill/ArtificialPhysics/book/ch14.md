## Shared model: the generalized LJ force law (§14.3, PDF pp. 474–475)

- Potential (Eq. 14.1): LJP(r) = 4ε[(σ/r)^12 − (σ/r)^6]. The well minimum is at r = 2^(1/6)σ. Fig. 14.1 plots it with ε = σ = 1.
- Generalized force (Eq. 14.4): **F_ij = 24ε ( d·σ^12 / r^13 − c·σ^6 / r^7 )**, with |F| ≤ Fmax. The desired spacing is D = 2^(1/6)σ.
  - ε sets the strength. c weights attraction and d weights repulsion.
  - **Eq. 14.3 has a typo:** it reads "−12σ^12/r^13 + 6σ^6/r^7" with the 7 misplaced. Use Eq. 14.4.
- There are three separate laws, each with its own (ε, c, d, Fmax), plus a global friction Fr. That makes **13 evolved genes** (p. 476): εr, cr, dr, Fmaxr, εo, co, do, Fmaxo, εg, cg, dg, Fmaxg, Fr.
- **Evolved values:** the chapter text gives none. The only published numbers are on the simulator screenshot in Ch. 15 (Fig. 15.1, PDF p. 519), which uses "optimal parameter settings… evolved… by Hettiarachchi":

  | interaction | ε | c (attr.) | d (rep.) | Fmax |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | agent–agent (A–A) | 6.75 | 1.43 | 2.29 | 1.08 |
  | agent–obstacle (A–O) | 8.99 | 4.45 | 9.38 | 4.32 |
  | agent–goal (A–G) | 10.72 | 5.25 | 5.19 | 1.87 |

  The same panel shows Agent # 40, Obstacle # 90 and Max Velocity 1.20. Friction is not shown.
- **Check (my computation):** with D = 50 (σ = 44.545) and the A–A values above:
  - F crosses zero at r = σ(d/c)^(1/6) = 48.2.
  - The force is clipped at Fmax = 1.08 for r ≲ 46.
  - Peak attraction is ≈ 0.65 at r ≈ 54, falling to 0.08 at r = 80.
- **The figures do not match those values.**
  - Fig. 14.3 (PDF p. 481, robot–robot law; sign convention is repulsion negative, attraction positive) shows a flat −4 for r < ~47, a sharp rise through zero at ~48–50, a peak of ≈ +2 at r ≈ 55, and decay to ≈ +0.3 at r = 80. The text says Fmaxr "takes effect when the distance … is less than 45" (p. 480).
  - So Fig. 14.3 has the same shape but about 3–4× the magnitude of the Fig. 15.1 numbers. It may come from a different run or scale.
- **Fig. 14.4 (robot–obstacle law, PDF p. 481):** flat ≈ −4.3 (Fmaxo) for distance < 14 (measured from the obstacle surface; the x-axis runs 0–30), rising to ≈ 0 by distance ~20.
  - The text says the repulsion "diminishes" beyond 14, and the sensing cutoff is Ro + 20 from the obstacle centre.
- **Fig. 14.5 (robot–goal law, PDF p. 482):** constant ≈ +1.7 (= Fmaxg) at every distance. The evolved goal law degenerated into a constant pull, so the goal never causes clustering.

## Entry 14.1 — Offline evolutionary learning (EL) of the three LJ laws (§14.4, PDF pp. 475–482)

**What it shows:** an EA finds LJ parameters that let a swarm cross a random obstacle field to a goal while keeping a hexagonal lattice.

**Agents:** 40 point robots in training, with radius 1. The simulator supports up to 100 robots and 100 obstacles.

**World (offline environment, p. 479):**
- 2D area of 900 × 700 units, with no stated boundary condition.
- **90 circular obstacles, Ro = 10**, placed at random but kept 50 units clear of the robot start area and of the goal. They cover about 4.5% of the area.
- One square **goal of 20 × 20**, placed at a random location on the right side. Robots start in the bottom-left area.
- **D = 50, Vmax = 20 units/s.**
- Sensing ranges:
  - robots sense other robots within 1.5D = 75;
  - robots sense obstacles within Ro + 20 = 30 of the obstacle centre;
  - the goal is sensed at any distance.
- A **collision** occurs when the robot centre is closer than Ro + 1 to the obstacle centre.
- A robot has **reached the goal** when it is within 4D = 200 of the goal centre.

**EA (pp. 475–480):**
- Real-valued chromosome of 13 genes with upper and lower bounds. The bounds are said to be in "Table 14.1", but that table actually only illustrates Gaussian mutation, so **the bounds are not given**.
- Gaussian mutation with mean 0 and a "predetermined" σ, also not given.
- One-point crossover and fitness-proportional selection.
- G generations, with G not given. The population size and the time limit are also not given.

**Fitness:** penalty = w1·P_Collision + w2·P_NoCohesion + w3·P_NotReachGoal, with **w1 = w2 = w3 = 1/3** (footnote, p. 478).
- Collision penalty: added at every time step for every robot inside an obstacle.
- Cohesion penalty: added at every time step for every robot, proportional to |#neighbours at distance D − 6|. "At distance D" needs a tolerance, which is not given.
- Goal penalty: added at the end if fewer than 80% of robots are within 4D of the goal by the time limit.
- Each individual is run on n random environments, and fitness = C − (R1 + … + Rn)/n for a large constant C (Eq. 14.5). C and n are not given.

**Qualitative result:**
- Before learning, the swarm performs poorly (Fig. 14.2, PDF p. 480).
- After learning, the formation behaves "like a viscous fluid": it keeps its hexagonal structure but deforms and flows around obstacles.

**Results to reproduce:** Table 14.2 (PDF p. 484). These are test-module results, averaged over 50 runs, **with 100 obstacles**:

| Robots | 20 | 40 | 60 | 80 | 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Collisions (# robots that hit) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 4 |
| Connectivity (min # robots still connected) | 12 | 22 | 36 | 54 | 67 |
| Reachability (% reaching goal) | 99 | 98 | 98 | 98 | 98 |
| Time to goal (80% arrived) | 530 | 570 | 610 | 650 | 690 |

- The swarm was trained with 40 robots but the behaviour scales to 100.
- The text does not say whether "time to goal" is in steps or seconds.

**BPP conversion:**
1. Start with a 2D particle replica that reuses the existing Ch. 3 LJ code. Groundwork needed:
   - three separate force classes with separate (ε, c, d, Fmax) each;
   - an obstacle force measured from the obstacle centre with cutoff Ro + 20;
   - a constant goal pull;
   - a Vmax clamp of 20;
   - the collision, cohesion and goal metrics.
2. Use the Fig. 15.1 parameters as the "evolved" law and check that you get viscous-fluid flow and roughly the Table 14.2 numbers. This is easy to medium.
3. Re-running the EA itself needs:
   - a headless batch mode with many environments per individual;
   - an outer GA loop, probably in Lua, or in an external script that drives BPP.
   This is medium to hard because of compute: population × n environments × time limit.
4. ★ **3D:** fly the evolved law with quadcopters through a random field of spheres or vertical columns, either as a 3D "viscous fluid" or as a 2.5D layer at fixed altitude.
   - LJ already settles on the drones, so this is the natural next step after the Ch. 3 LJ goal and obstacle demo.
   - Hexagonal cohesion (6 neighbours) becomes 12 neighbours in a 3D FCC/HCP lattice.

**Ambiguities the porter must decide:**
- Fmax scaling: Fig. 14.3 versus Fig. 15.1.
- Friction value.
- Time limit.
- Whether obstacle distance is measured from the centre or the surface. The force-law plot looks surface-relative; the sensing range is from the centre.
- Sign convention: Eq. 14.4 is positive = repulsive, while the plots are negative = repulsive.

## Entry 14.2 — Fig. 14.6: 50 robots flow around one large obstacle (PDF p. 483)

**What it shows:** four snapshots of 50 robots using the evolved LJ law around **one large obstacle** that is bigger than the formation's neighbour spacing. In the image it looks like a dark blob, roughly square.
1. A fully connected lattice approaches the obstacle.
2. The swarm splits into two fronts, keeping **100% connectivity**.
3. The fronts merge again.
4. The swarm arrives at the goal as a cohesive lattice.

**Result to reproduce:** the qualitative split-and-merge with no disconnection.

**BPP conversion:** easy as a particle demo, and a good showcase. ★ In 3D with drones, the swarm can also flow over or under the obstacle, not only around it.

## Entry 14.3 — Offline law tested in the online corridor (first DAEDALUS attempt), Fig. 14.9 (§14.6, PDF pp. 485–488)

**Online environment (p. 486):**
- 2D area of 1600 × 950 units: a "long corridor" with **5 goals** at random positions (Fig. 14.7).
- A different obstacle course lies between each pair of goals, with **90 obstacles** in total.
- **Obstacle radius 30**, covering more than 16% of the area (3× the offline coverage).
- **Vmax = 30** (1.5× offline). Everything else is as offline.
- 60 robots (Fig. 14.8).
- Rules for moving on:
  - robots left behind in cul-de-sacs do not continue to the next goal;
  - robots that collided but reached the goal do continue, "repaired at the goal".

**DAEDALUS setup:**
- Each robot starts with a copy of the offline-optimized law, mutated at a **1% mutation rate** (Gaussian).
- Initial "worthiness" (fitness) is **1000** for every robot.
- Robots with higher fitness give their rules to robots with lower fitness. Recombination and mutation perturb the rules.

**Results (Fig. 14.9, PDF p. 488):**
- Survival in an environment like the offline one: about 98% after 5 goals (≈ 59 of 60).
- Survival in the online environment: **51.66%** after 5 goals. Read from the plot, about 31 of 60, falling almost linearly by goal.
- Collision rates are low.
- The offline law never learned to avoid cul-de-sacs, so robots get stuck.

**Not specified:**
- the exact fitness and reward update rule (what raises or lowers worthiness);
- when and how rule transfer happens (neighbour range, trigger);
- whether mutation is applied continuously or on events.

The text only says: "robots are penalized if they collide with obstacles and/or if they leave their neighbors behind." The published papers [92, 93] would be needed.

**BPP conversion:** medium. It needs:
- a goal sequence (5 goals);
- per-robot heterogeneous parameter sets;
- left-behind detection, i.e. a robot not moving or not reaching the goal before the swarm moves on;
- a rule for when the swarm advances to the next goal. This is not specified; for example, after a time limit or after 80% have arrived.

## Entry 14.4 — Obstructed perception: line-of-sight between robots (§14.7, PDF pp. 489–490)

**What it shows:** a large obstacle blocks robot–robot sensing (Fig. 14.10). This is a perception model, not an experiment on its own.

**Model:**
- A and B are robots and C is the obstacle centre with radius r.
- The minimum distance from C to segment AB is minD = |(1−q)A + qB − C|, where q = ((C−A)·(B−A)) / |B−A|² (Eqs. 14.6–14.7), clamped to 0 ≤ q ≤ 1.
- Perception is obstructed if r > minD.
- A line of sight that only touches the obstacle edge still counts as visible.
- Only robot–robot sensing is blocked; obstacle and goal sensing are not.

**BPP conversion:** easy as a geometric segment–circle test.
- In BPP it is better done with Bullet ray tests (`rayTest`) against obstacle bodies. That gives true line of sight in 3D for free.
- This is groundwork reused by Ch. 15 and likely by Ch. 6/12, and probably also wanted for the "chains" and "coverage" ports.

## Entry 14.5 — Survival through cooperation: mutation-rate classes, Approaches 1 and 2 (§14.8, §14.8.1, PDF pp. 491–497)

**Setup:**
- Online corridor (Entry 14.3) with obstructed perception, 60 agents.
- Five groups of 12 agents with mutation rates **1%, 3%, 5%, 7%, 9%**.
- Results are averaged over **25 runs**.
- The control studies are named train–test by obstacle size: small = offline Ro 10, large = online Ro 30.

**Two optional heuristics:**
- **Approach 1:** if agent i is not moving (blocked) and neighbour j is moving, i copies j's agent–agent force law **and** j's mutation rate.
- **Approach 2:** at a goal, agent i adopts the mean mutation rate of those neighbours (r ≤ 1.5D) that have higher worthiness than i, and uses it to reach the next goal.

**Control curves (Figs. 14.11, 14.12; values read from the plots unless stated):**
- No DAEDALUS (small–small): **93.3%** survive to goal 5, i.e. 56 of 60 (text).
- No DAEDALUS (small–large): ≈ 24 of 60 by goal 5.
- No DAEDALUS (large–large): ≈ 22–23 of 60.

**Survivors to goal 5 out of 60, by experiment:**

| Experiment | Survivors at goal 5 | Survivors per goal (start, 1–5) | Per-rate breakdown |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) DAEDALUS small–large, no approaches (Fig. 14.11, Table 14.3, PDF pp. 492–493) | **29 = 48.3%**; better than large–large training | 60, 54, 50, 39, 35, 29 | at goal 5: 1%→6, 3%→4, **5%→9**, 7%→7, 9%→3 |
| (b) + Approach 1 (Fig. 14.12, Table 14.4, PDF p. 494) | **37 = 61.6%**; +13.3 points over (a), significant by two-tailed t-test at α = 0.05 | 60, 54, 47, 42, 39, 37 | at goal 5: 5, 6, 10, 9, 7. 5% and 7% are favoured, but the rate distribution changes little. |
| (c) Approaches 1 + 2 (Fig. 14.13, Table 14.5, PDF pp. 495–496) | **41 = 68.3%**; significant vs (a) | 60, 54, 51, 48, 45, 41 | rates collapse into bins: at goal 2 the bins ≤1/≤3/≤5/≤7/≤9 hold 1/1/27/22/0; at goal 5 they hold 0/2/24/15/0 |
| (d) Approach 2 only (Fig. 14.14, Table 14.6, PDF p. 497) | **34 = 56.6%** | 60, 53, 44, 40, 37, 34 | at goal 5 the bins hold 0/0/17/17/0 |

- **Conclusion:** each approach helps, and the two combined help most. Mutation rates of 3–7% are best.
- **Note on (c) and (d):** in Tables 14.5–14.6 the columns are mutation-rate *ranges* (3% < μ ≤ 5% and so on), not the initial groups. Agents migrate between bins because they adopt neighbours' mean rates. The row totals do add up (for example, goal 2 in Table 14.5: 1+1+27+22+0 = 51).

**BPP conversion:**
- Medium, once Entry 14.3 exists.
- Needs per-agent state (rules, mutation rate, worthiness), a "not moving" test (velocity below a threshold for some time), and neighbour messaging within 1.5D gated by line of sight.
- Needs a batch harness for 25 runs per configuration and statistics.
- The worthiness update rule is unspecified, so the porter must invent one. Suggestion: subtract per collision step and per left-behind event.

## Contradictions and additions to the existing plan

1. **"Daedalus (Ch. 14)" is in the plan, but Ch. 14 is not self-contained.**
   - The worthiness/reward update, the rule-transfer trigger and range (only Approach 1 and 2 are defined), the EA bounds, mutation σ, population size, generations, time limit and friction are all missing.
   - Evolved parameter values appear only in Ch. 15's Fig. 15.1, and they disagree in scale with Fig. 14.3.
   - The porter must invent the missing pieces or consult refs [90, 92, 93] (Hettiarachchi's thesis and papers).
2. **Order of work.** Do the offline LJ obstacle-course law (Entries 14.1–14.2) first. It is essentially an extension of the already-done Ch. 3 LJ goal and obstacle demo, with three separate force laws and a constant goal pull, and it is directly reusable by the Ch. 15 scouts port. DAEDALUS layers on top.
3. **New groundwork the plan does not list:**
   - line-of-sight occlusion between agents (Bullet ray tests);
   - multi-goal corridors;
   - per-agent heterogeneous parameter sets;
   - a batch/statistics harness with 25–50 runs per cell;
   - an optional GA loop.
4. **LJ on drones.** LJ already settles on the drones, so ★ "Daedalus drones in a 3D column forest" is feasible. Vmax rises 20 → 30 in the online test, which interacts with drone dynamics and needs a speed limit in the waypoint generator.
5. **Hybrid LJ→gas escape (Ch. 7 + Ch. 14)** is a cheap extra demo that the plan does not list.
