- PDF pages 254–281 (book pp. 223–249). Book page = PDF page − 31.
- Nature of chapter: **pure theory**. No experiments, no NetLogo model, no parameter tables. It defines

## E1. CPT task decomposition and the robot-swarm-as-finite-difference-grid idea (§8.2, pp. 225–233 / PDF 256–264)

- CPT = inverse problem; the forward problem (CFD plume) is only needed in simulation (p. 228).
- Three subtasks (§8.2.3, p. 229–231): (1) detection = **casting** (zigzag or spiral undirected search,
  until chemical above sensor threshold); (2) tracing; (3) emitter identification. The chapter treats
  (2) and (3) only.
- Five prior tracing classes listed (p. 230): heuristic, chemotaxis (follow ∇ρ), anemotaxis (go upwind),
  hybrid, infotaxis (puff frequency / stochastic). Only chemotaxis and anemotaxis are compared.
- Eulerian vs Lagrangian (§8.2.4.1, p. 231): Ch. 7 (kinetic theory) = robots ARE the fluid (Lagrangian);
  Ch. 6 (bioluminescence) and CPT = robots move THROUGH the fluid (Eulerian). CPT adopts Eulerian.
- Swarm = mobile sensing grid; each robot measures ρ (chemical sensor) and V (anemometer), shares with
  neighbours; derivatives by **second-order central differences** [291] (p. 233). No leader, no global
  broadcast.
- Assumptions (p. 226): chemical velocity = carrier-fluid velocity; fluid compressible; robots do not
  occlude each other or perturb the flow; sensing noise ignored (footnote 3, p. 239: "sensor and actuator
  noise are not included").
- Multiple emitters split the swarm into sub-swarms (footnote 1, p. 229, ref [292]) — not shown.

**BPP notes.** Groundwork shared by all CPT entries (Ch. 8 and 9):
1. A scalar field ρ(x,y,z,t) and vector field V(x,y,z,t) the Lua script can sample at any point
   (analytic first; puff model of Ch. 9 next). Needs field drawing (coloured voxels / point sprites /
   slice plane) — "scalar-field drawing" groundwork.
2. Per-agent sensor read with optional noise and threshold.
3. Neighbour message passing of (ρ, V, position) within sensing radius.
4. A finite-difference estimator on an irregular neighbour set (least-squares gradient, or central
   difference on lattice neighbours).
Effort: medium (pure Lua, no new vehicle). ★ The robots already fly in 3D in BPP, so the 3D form of every
formula in this chapter (the chapter states them in 3D) can be used directly — Ch. 9 only did 2D.

## E2. Emitter identification by surface flux integral (§8.4.1, pp. 236–237 / PDF 267–268)

- Divergence of mass flux at p (Eq. 8.2): ∇·(ρV)(p) = lim_{W→p} ∮_A (ρV)·n̂ dA / |W|.
- Practical test: robots surround a suspected emitter; source if ∮ (ρV)·n̂ dA > 0, **sustained for τ
  time units** (τ "experimentally determined", no value given) to reject transient turbulent puffs.
- After surrounding, the lattice locates the emitter by moving opposite to the direction of flux outflow.
- Emitter types mentioned: continuous, single-puff, regular/irregular pulsing; emitter size/shape/height
  matter but are not parameterised.

**BPP notes.** Implement as a closed ring (2D) or closed shell (3D, ★ e.g. drones on an icosahedron /
cube-lattice shell around a suspected point) computing Σ ρ_i (V_i·n̂_i) ΔA_i. Check: positive and
steady around the true emitter, sign-flipping around turbulent puffs. Porter must choose τ and the
threshold. Easy once the field exists. ★ A 3D shell of UAVs closing around an elevated stack emitter is
the natural 3D version (ground robots cannot do this).

## E3. Fluxotaxis navigation law — GDMF (§8.4.2, pp. 238–240 / PDF 269–271)

- Divergence of mass flux in 3D:
  ∇·(ρV) = u ∂ρ/∂x + ρ ∂u/∂x + v ∂ρ/∂y + ρ ∂v/∂y + w ∂ρ/∂z + ρ ∂w/∂z.
- Navigation vector: **GDMF = ∇[∇·(ρV)]** — points toward sources, away from sinks.
- Each agent: environmental force from GDMF + formation cohesion forces from range/bearing to neighbours;
  move along the vector sum. (No gains, no Fmax, no lattice law given in Ch. 8; Ch. 9 supplies LJ.)
- Decomposition (Eq. 8.3, p. 241): ∇·(ρV) = ρ(∇·V) + V·(∇ρ). First term ≈ anemotaxis-like (dominates in
  divergent flow); second term ≈ chemotaxis-like (dominates when ∇·V = 0). Switching is automatic.
- **Theorem 8.1 (Source):** GDMF robots always move toward a chemical source. **Theorem 8.2 (Sink):** always
  away from a sink. Proofs in [232] (Zarzhitsky thesis), not in book. Guarantees are local only.
- Practical rules: below a density threshold (far from source, or solenoidal/incompressible regions where
  ρ is invariant along streamlines) all CPT methods fail → fall back to **casting** (p. 240).
- Claims (no data in this chapter): urban environment with buildings — fluxotaxis succeeds, chemo-/
  anemotaxis "flounder and fail" [2]; the swarm-size effect is in Ch. 9.

**BPP notes.** This is the core algorithm to port; computed per agent at the AP rate (5 Hz like existing
formations). GDMF needs a *second* derivative, so each agent needs either (a) neighbours' divergence values
(two-hop: each agent computes local ∇·(ρV) from its neighbours, broadcasts it, then takes the gradient of
neighbours' divergences), or (b) a stencil reaching ±2 cells. Choose (a): it is what a lattice naturally
does. Output a goal force F_goal = k·GDMF/|GDMF| (unit vector, as Ch. 9 does) added to the LJ formation
force, then waypoint for drones. Porter must choose the gain k and clamp (book gives none). Medium.

## E4. Anemotaxis vs fluxotaxis near the emitter (§8.5.1, pp. 241–242 / PDF 272–273)

- Anemotaxis vector: −V (speed constant or ∝ |V|), used only when ρ above threshold.
- Qualitative prediction: with an ambient cross-wind passing the emitter, anemotaxis follows the
  ambient wind and **overshoots** the emitter; fluxotaxis ignores the ambient wind (not a source) and
  homes on the emitter. "Observed many times" — results in Ch. 9 (§9.5.1.4, §9.5.2.4).

**BPP notes.** Small demonstration: uniform wind + emitter; run both laws from same start; plot track.
Must reproduce overshoot/oscillation for anemotaxis and stop-at-emitter for fluxotaxis. Easy given E3.

## E5. Continuous emitter, diffusion-dominated: closed-form check (§8.5.3, pp. 244–246 / PDF 275–277)

Setup: number density n(r,t), ρ = m n; diffusion equation ∂n/∂t = D∇²n (Eq. 8.4; D here = diffusion
coefficient, NOT the lattice spacing). Constant source rate S_o at r = 0, N_p = t S_o. Imposed radial
velocity V = V_o r̂.
- Steady state (Eq. 8.5): **n(r,∞) = S_o / (4πDr)** (1/r decay, Fig. 8.2).
- Fluxotaxis (Eq. 8.6): **GDMF = −(m V_o S_o)/(2πD r³) r̂** → unit vector −r̂ (correct).
- Chemotaxis (Eq. 8.7): **DG = ∇ρ = −S_o/(4πD r²) r̂** (as printed; it omits the factor m) → −r̂ (correct).
- Result to reproduce: both point at the source; |GDMF| ∝ r⁻³ grows faster than |DG| ∝ r⁻² as r → 0.
- I re-derived Eq. 8.6 and it is correct in 3D: ∇·(ρV) = mV_o(n′ + 2n/r) = mV_oS_o/(4πDr²); its r-derivative
  gives −mV_oS_o/(2πDr³).

**BPP notes.** Analytic 3D field, no CFD. Place a 3D lattice (particles or drones) at many radii, have
each compute GDMF and DG by finite differences from neighbours' samples, compare against the formulas
(direction error, magnitude vs r on log–log: slopes −3 and −2). Good unit test for the E3 estimator.
Easy. ★ Decide 2D vs 3D: in a 2D plane the 3D formulas do NOT carry over (2D divergence of f r̂ is
f′ + f/r, and the 2D steady continuous source has no 1/r steady state, it grows logarithmically). Either
sample the 3D field in a horizontal slice (ground robots tracing an airborne plume, as the chapter
intends) or run fully 3D with drones.

## E6. Single-puff emitter: GDMF vs chemotaxis (§8.5.4, pp. 246–249 / PDF 277–280)

- Solution (Eq. 8.8): **n(r,t) = N_p/(4πDt)^{3/2} · e^{−r²/4Dt}**; spread r ≈ √(4Dt); width Δr ∝ √t (Fig. 8.3).
  V = V_o r̂ again. Let A = N_p/(4πDt)^{3/2}, B = 1/(4Dt).
- **GDMF = −2mV_oAB (3 + 1/(Br²) − 2Br²) e^{−Br²} r̂** (text is garbled in the PDF layer; I re-derived
  it and this is the correct form, consistent with Eq. 8.9).
- **DG = −2ABr e^{−Br²} r̂**.
- Claims to reproduce: inside inflection point r_i = 1/√(2B), GDMF direction = −r̂;
  lim_{r→r_i} |GDMF| = 8mV_oAB e^{−1/2} > 0 (Eq. 8.9); lim_{r→0} |GDMF| → ∞ (Eq. 8.10);
  lim_{r→0} |DG| = 0 (chemotaxis signal vanishes at the emitter). Predictive region grows as √t.
- Additional fact (my derivation, not in the book): GDMF actually stays pointing inward until
  Br² = (3+√17)/4 ≈ 1.78, i.e. r ≈ 1.33/√B ≈ 1.9 r_i; beyond that it points AWAY from the source. A port
  should show this sign flip; it also means fluxotaxis needs casting/other cues in the puff's outer tail.
- Intuition given: GDMF uses second derivatives (Taylor-series argument).

**BPP notes.** Same harness as E5 with a time-varying field; animate the Gaussian growing and show the
GDMF/DG arrows on a lattice. Easy. ★ With noisy sensors the r⁻³ / 1/r² singularity near the emitter will
dominate; add noise to show how finite-difference GDMF degrades (noise is explicitly excluded by the
book, footnote 3 — a port can add it as an extension).
