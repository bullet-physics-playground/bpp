## Gomboc Variety (uniform-density Gömböcs)

**What it shows** — A Gömböc is a convex body of uniform density with exactly one stable and one unstable equilibrium, so however it is set down it rolls back to the same upright pose. The table drops up to 12 bodies at random orientations, cycling through three shapes: the real Gömböc (Gomboc-C, at 9 cm and at 75% and 125% size) and two of M. L. Sloan's analytic Gömböcs (β = 0.07 and 0.08). The lesson is that having one resting point in theory is not enough. Gomboc-C rights itself every time, while the gently sloped Sloan shapes usually stall on their sides under ordinary rolling resistance.

**Model** — Bullet handles contact. Each body is a `btConvexHullShape`:
- **Gomboc-C:** the convex hull of 15,548 points on the GOMBOC900 CAD surfaces (since 19 Oct 2026; before, 13,476): a 1.2 mm tessellation of every face except the bottom cylinder it rests and rocks on, plus 1229 whole straight lines (rulings) across that cylinder, 30 µm apart at the bottom. Made from the STEP file by exhibits/Gomboc/hull-reconstruction/gen_hull.py (README.txt there). The CAD model is GOMBOC900, made in SolidWorks 2010 by Kiss Attila (organisation "e.v.") on 12 December 2013, according to its STEP file's header (FILE_NAME author and organisation). It was published as "Gömböc" Convex, homogeneous three-dimensional body by Istenes József on GrabCAD on 5 December 2024 (https://grabcad.com/library/gomboc-convex-homogeneous-three-dimensional-body-1). Its licence is not stated in the download and was not checked; the STEP file is not in the repository. (Until 19 Oct 2026 the "Kiss Attila, 2013" credit was marked unconfirmed; the STEP header confirms it.)
- **Sloan shapes:** hull points of r⁴ = 1 + 4β sinφ cos(θ − 5φ).
- **Margin:** 0.04 cm, scaled with size.
- **Mass and inertia:** exact (CAD or analytic) at resin density 1.2 g/cm³. That gives 323 g at 9 cm, 136 g at 75%, 630 g at 125%, and 407 g / 401 g for β = 0.07 / 0.08.

The script steps the world itself at 1200 steps per simulated second (20 substeps of 1/1200 s per 1/60 s frame), using Bullet's semi-implicit Euler. Every substep it applies two losses:
- rolling resistance, a couple of at most M = δ·m·g, with lever arm δ = 150 µm by default, handed to Bullet as a torque for the step (`demo/module/rolling.lua`, since 20 Oct 2026): kinetic, M against the rolling; static, once the rolling has stopped (reversed within a step), whatever couple balances the moment of the table's push where Bullet pushed (the centre of its contact impulses, averaged over 0.02 s) and stops any turning left, up to M. So a body stays put while gravity's lever is under δ and rolls again when it is over;
- spin friction of 0.7 rad/s², a cut in the spin about the vertical before the substep.

Other settings: friction 0.5 (Bullet multiplies it by the table's 0.8: 0.4 at the table, 0.25 body on body), restitution 0.1 (table 0.2: 0.02 at the table, 0.01 body on body), air drag 0, no taps. The tray is flat with 8° ramps at the edges and a rim. Status thresholds: "Upright" means within 15° of the resting normal for 2 s. "At rest" means less than 0.2° of turn and 0.02 cm of movement over 2 s. Five seconds after a body comes to rest it is pulled away (up and off to the side, out of view) while the next shape is let down from above its drop spot and dropped (GV_REDROP); see the 18 Oct 2026 note below.

**Uniform-density monostable polyhedra (checked 18 Oct 2026; not exhibited)** — Uniform-density convex polyhedra that rest on only one face do exist: Conway and Guy's 19-face body (1969), Bezdek's 18-face body (2011) and Reshetov's 14-face body (2014). But they work only by micrometres. Their published coordinates were scaled to 200 mm and checked with exact rational arithmetic (scratch study `poly-uniform/`: `statics.py`, `robust.py`, `cg_exact.py`). The smallest margin, meaning how far the centre-of-mass foot lies outside the nearest false face, is:
- Reshetov 14, 15, 16 and 17 faces: 2.2, 10.6, 7.0 and 15.1 µm;
- Bezdek (Regős's lattice version): 7.2 µm;
- the study's own refit of a Conway–Guy-type 19-face body: 79 µm.

In 300 simulated prints per body, with every face plane randomly off by 0.05 mm, **0%** of builds stayed monostable for all six. A realistic combination of FDM errors gives 0–0.3%.

A side finding: the integer Conway–Guy lattice coordinates published by Regős (2025, Table 4) do **not** come out monostable when computed. They give 15 resting faces, with the centroid on the wrong side for Conway's argument. Rebuilding the cross-section from the paper's own construction parameters (θ = 20°, δ = 0.5°) reproduces the table to 1.5 units in 2.8 million, so this is not a transcription error on our side. With the inclined planes made steeper (slope m ≥ 15 instead of 4) the same construction does give one resting face. We do not know which is intended. The data and scripts are in the scratch study `poly-uniform/` (data.py, statics_200.txt, regos_pc_check.txt, robust_table.md). This is why the monostable polyhedra in Bille Drop are **weighted**: the 26- and 37-corner bodies have tungsten corner weights, and the PLA-only body has a solid base under an air void. Their margins are millimetres, not micrometres. None of the uniform bodies was added to an exhibit.

**Known departures and honest caveats**
- **Changed 20 Oct 2026: rolling resistance is a couple through Bullet's step, and holds (Gömböc Variety and Gomboc Drop C).** Old: before each step the rolling part of the turning lost δ·g/(k²+r²)·Δt, about the point straight below the centre of mass, and could not reverse. A cut in speed before the step cannot cancel what the step itself adds: Bullet adds gravity and the table's push inside the step and moves the body with the speed they leave, so a body that should be held rolled on at a·Δt, a = g·lever/(k²+r²) (the cause suspected on 19 Oct, now shown). Test scenes (scratch `rr/rr-test.lua`: the gomboc's mass, inertia and 1.147 mm offset of the centre of mass under a 3.6 cm sphere, cylinder or the real hull, set down at rest at a tilt, δ = 150 µm): on the sphere (one point of contact, lever 100 µm at 5°) the old model crept 0.049 / 0.029 / 0.016 / 0.0064°/s at 600 / 1200 / 2400 / 4800 steps/s, in proportion to the step and 1.0–1.3× a·Δt (1.4–1.7× with the body turned 90°: see below); step by step the turning was cut to 0 before the step and came out at gravity's a·Δt after it, and the tilt moved by exactly that. New: δ·m·g against the rolling as a torque (btRigidBody:applyTorque) before each stepSimulation, so it enters the same solve as gravity; when the rolling reverses within a step it is stopped, and from then on the torque balances the moment of the table's push (from v:eachContact: the centre of the contact impulses Bullet applied, averaged over 0.02 s because Bullet shares the load among a line of contact points differently each step) and stops what turning is left, up to δ·m·g; when the push's moment is over δ·m·g it rolls again. The test sphere is then held to under 0.001° in 30 s at 1200 and 4800 steps/s, rolling along x or z, with the averaging time anywhere from 0.005 to 0.1 s. (A first version that held only about the axis it had been rolling about failed on the sphere at 4800 steps/s, 0.012°/s: a point contact can roll either way.) One Gomboc-C dropped alone (Drop C, GC_COUNT=1 GC_REDROP=0, 140 s, GC_SEED=1–7, GC_LOG), tilt change 30–140 s after the drop: old 0.0004 (rested at 0.4°), 0.009, 0.019, 0.021, 0.023, 0.029, 0.030°/s, steadily home; new 0.00002, 0.00003, 0.00005, 0.0002, 0.0008°/s, and two that moved after their first at-rest report and then held: 2.68° → 1.45° between 30 and 60 s, and 0.45° → 1.01° (away from upright, so not gravity) between 20 and 80 s. Rest tilts 0.4–7.2°. Slide over the same 110 s: new 0.4–3.2 mm a minute, old 0.01–1.2: the hold does not stop Bullet's sliding at rest and in some runs it slides more (item below). Not changed: spin friction (nothing drives a spin at rest). Effects: rests lie further from upright (no creep home), still all inside the 7.51° / 10.04° / 6.00° bound; times to rest and tallies within run-to-run spread; some Sloan rests now count as upright (above); +4% CPU.
- **What still moves a body at rest is Bullet's contact (found 20 Oct 2026, not changed).** (i) Line contacts: on the 3.6 cm cylinder (exact btCylinderShape or the hull's rulings) Bullet keeps up to 4 contact points, some left from earlier steps, and shares the load among them differently each step. The lever across the contact line of the centre of Bullet's contact impulses, logged every step at rest: Gomboc-C hull at 3°, true lever 57–60 µm, Bullet's 68–85 µm on average, 1–166 µm step to step; exact cylinder at 5°, true 80–100 µm, Bullet's 102–270 µm on average, up to 600 µm. That is as large as δ, so on line contacts no rolling-resistance law in the script can hold to better than this: test cylinders and hulls still drift 0.001–0.02°/s and now and then break away; the hull's nearly flat bottom puts every ruling within 1 µm of the table over a band about 0.5 mm wide, so its contact point is ill-defined. (ii) Bullet uses one friction direction per contact (solver mode default), along the slip; at rest (slip under √FLT_EPSILON = 3.5 µm/s here) it falls back to world x, so friction can't stop slip along z in that step: a body rolling along z pivots about its centre of mass in one step (3.1× the rolling response, (k²+r²)/k²) and the next step's friction pulls it back. That amplified the old creep 1.4–1.7× and lets bodies slide at rest (0.4–3.2 mm a minute for one Gomboc-C with the new couple, 0.01–1.2 with the old; why the couple slides more in some runs was not found). A test build with SOLVER_USE_2_FRICTION_DIRECTIONS removed the pivoting but not (i). The solver settings involved (solver mode, split-impulse threshold) are not reachable from Lua; a small bpp change exposing them was proposed, not applied.
- **Changed 19 Oct 2026: Gomboc-C's hull is fine along its resting trough (Gömböc Variety and Gomboc Drop C).** Old: the convex hull of a 1.2 mm tessellation of all the CAD surfaces, 13,476 points, now reproduced exactly from the STEP file by hull-reconstruction/gen_hull.py (gmsh 4.15.2, MeshSizeMax 12 mm, hull vertices rounded in metres, hull again). New: the same tessellation everywhere except the bottom cylinder the body rests and rocks on (radius 3.6 cm, axis along body x), whose inside nodes are replaced by 1229 whole straight lines (rulings) across it, 30 µm apart at the bottom (spacing chosen so that for any rolling lever arm up to 300 µm at any size from 75% the hull can hold a tilt at most ~0.75° beyond the smooth cylinder, and further up no hold at all), 15,548 points, all on the CAD surfaces. The render mesh is the old one, moved by the change in the centre of mass. - **Rolling resistance and spin friction are script-side models.** They are constant moments (rolling resistance a couple of at most δ·m·g since 20 Oct 2026), not Bullet's own rolling friction (which in Bullet 3.24 limits each manifold's rolling friction by one contact point's push only). δ = 150 µm and 0.7 rad/s² were chosen, not measured. The findings PDF (Table 5b) shows that the outcome depends strongly on δ: without rolling resistance nothing ever settles, Gomboc-C included.

- **New hull: a resting Gomboc-C creeps toward upright** (fixed 20 Oct 2026: see the first item; kept as the record). One Gomboc-C (Drop C, GC_COUNT=1 GC_SEED=1 GC_REDROP=0, 140 s, GC_LOG): on the old hull it came to rest at 1.35° and stayed at 1.346–1.350° for 125 s (on a facet, whose foot lies inside it). Gravity's lever there is under δ, so a real body would not move: this is numerical. The rolling resistance cancels the turning speed before each step, but within a step gravity adds some and the step moves the body with it (semi-implicit Euler), about d·sin(tilt)·g·Δt/(k²+r²) per second, 0.016°/s at 3°: the right size, but at 2400 and 4800 steps/s the measured creep (0.03–0.08 and 0.007–0.04°/s at 1.5–3.5°, one run each) does not fall in proportion, so the contact solver adds to it. The old hull hid it: its facets stop the body on the nearest resting face. Not changed (it would change the loss model); a fix would cancel the speed the step itself adds while the lever is under δ.
