## Gomboc Variety (uniform-density Gömböcs)

**What it shows** — A Gömböc is a convex body of uniform density with exactly one stable and one unstable equilibrium, so however it is set down it rolls back to the same upright pose. The table drops up to 12 bodies at random orientations, cycling through three shapes: the real Gömböc (Gomboc-C, at 9 cm and at 75% and 125% size) and two of M. L. Sloan's analytic Gömböcs (β = 0.07 and 0.08). The lesson is that having one resting point in theory is not enough. Gomboc-C rights itself every time, while the gently sloped Sloan shapes usually stall on their sides under ordinary rolling resistance.

**Model** — Bullet handles contact. Each body is a `btConvexHullShape`:
- **Gomboc-C:** the convex hull of a 1.2 mm tessellation of the GOMBOC900 CAD B-spline surfaces (13,476 points, per gomboc-drop-c.lua). The CAD model is "Gömböc" Convex, homogeneous three-dimensional body, by Istenes József, published on GrabCAD on 5 December 2024 (https://grabcad.com/library/gomboc-convex-homogeneous-three-dimensional-body-1). Its licence was not checked: the page could not be reached from the build machine. The code used to credit "Kiss Attila, 2013"; that credit came in with the first Gömböc commit (048ca2b, 9 Oct 2026) without the CAD file, its properties or any note, so its source is not known and it is not confirmed.
- **Sloan shapes:** hull points of r⁴ = 1 + 4β sinφ cos(θ − 5φ).
- **Margin:** 0.04 cm, scaled with size.
- **Mass and inertia:** exact (CAD or analytic) at resin density 1.2 g/cm³. That gives 323 g at 9 cm, 136 g at 75%, 630 g at 125%, and 407 g / 401 g for β = 0.07 / 0.08.

The script steps the world itself at 1200 steps per simulated second (20 substeps of 1/1200 s per 1/60 s frame), using Bullet's semi-implicit Euler. Before every substep it applies two losses as constant moments at the contact:
- rolling resistance M = δ·m·g, with lever arm δ = 150 µm by default;
- spin friction of 0.7 rad/s².

Other settings: friction 0.5 (Bullet multiplies it by the table's 0.8: 0.4 at the table, 0.25 body on body), restitution 0.1 (table 0.2: 0.02 at the table, 0.01 body on body), air drag 0, no taps. The tray is flat with 8° ramps at the edges and a rim. Status thresholds: "Upright" means within 15° of the resting normal for 2 s. "At rest" means less than 0.2° of turn and 0.02 cm of movement over 2 s. Five seconds after a body comes to rest it is pulled away (up and off to the side, out of view) while the next shape is let down from above its drop spot and dropped (GV_REDROP); see the 18 Oct 2026 note below.

**Uniform-density monostable polyhedra (checked 18 Oct 2026; not exhibited)** — Uniform-density convex polyhedra that rest on only one face do exist: Conway and Guy's 19-face body (1969), Bezdek's 18-face body (2011) and Reshetov's 14-face body (2014). But they work only by micrometres. Their published coordinates were scaled to 200 mm and checked with exact rational arithmetic (scratch study `poly-uniform/`: `statics.py`, `robust.py`, `cg_exact.py`). The smallest margin, meaning how far the centre-of-mass foot lies outside the nearest false face, is:
- Reshetov 14, 15, 16 and 17 faces: 2.2, 10.6, 7.0 and 15.1 µm;
- Bezdek (Regős's lattice version): 7.2 µm;
- the study's own refit of a Conway–Guy-type 19-face body: 79 µm.

In 300 simulated prints per body, with every face plane randomly off by 0.05 mm, **0%** of builds stayed monostable for all six. A realistic combination of FDM errors gives 0–0.3%.

A side finding: the integer Conway–Guy lattice coordinates published by Regős (2025, Table 4) do **not** come out monostable when computed. They give 15 resting faces, with the centroid on the wrong side for Conway's argument. Rebuilding the cross-section from the paper's own construction parameters (θ = 20°, δ = 0.5°) reproduces the table to 1.5 units in 2.8 million, so this is not a transcription error on our side. With the inclined planes made steeper (slope m ≥ 15 instead of 4) the same construction does give one resting face. We do not know which is intended. The data and scripts are in the scratch study `poly-uniform/` (data.py, statics_200.txt, regos_pc_check.txt, robust_table.md). This is why the monostable polyhedra in Bille Drop are **weighted**: the 26- and 37-corner bodies have tungsten corner weights, and the PLA-only body has a solid base under an air void. Their margins are millimetres, not micrometres. None of the uniform bodies was added to an exhibit.

**Known departures and honest caveats**
- **Changed 18 Oct 2026: re-drops (Gömböc Variety and Gomboc Drop C).** In Variety the NEXT shape was swapped in at the old one's resting pose and carried up from there by the attendant's lift (a real rigid body moved by hand, which could push its neighbours), so it looked like the same body lifted and dropped again. Gomboc Drop C did not carry at all: it moved the same gömböc straight to its next drop pose. Now, in both, the old body is pulled away (up 120 cm and 120 cm to the side, out of view, then gone) while, at the same time, the new one is let down from 80 cm above its drop spot. Both are drawn by display-only copies that collide with nothing, and the new rigid body joins the world only at the moment it is let go, at the pose, velocity and spin it always had, from the same random numbers in the same order (Variety's dot goes with each copy). In Variety the move takes the same GRIP_S + LIFT_S = 1 s as the old lift, so each release is in the same frame as before. In Drop C the 1 s carry is new: each new gömböc is let go 1 s later than it used to appear, and its clock starts then. Drop C's start is unchanged (gömböcs new on the table are put straight at their drop poses). R and U with bodies on the table use the same swap. No physics parameter changed. Effect: Variety's seeded runs above are identical apart from the wording of the replacement line. In Drop C (GC_SEED=3, 4 gömböcs, re-drop 1 s) every run's own times are unchanged where its neighbours didn't matter (Red #2 upright 4.05 s, at rest 5.43 s; Yellow #2 13.03 / 15.22 s, as before), the timeline shifts by 1 s a replacement, and the 60 s tally happened to come out identical. With GC_SEED=1 and its defaults the runs drift apart after the first replacement: 60 s tally median 6.6 → 8.8 s, 12 runs, 0 not upright either way; 21 at-rest reports, all upright, tilt 0.3–11.6° (the old script: 0.3–11.4°). The 11.4–11.6° rests carry no "touching" flag, and they exceed the "0.3–8 deg" in gomboc-drop-c.lua's header. This was already so before the change; explained 19 Oct 2026 (next item).
- Fixed 15 Oct 2026: the attendant's lift ran during the start pause (a `holdTick` that called `liftStep`), so with the pause on the output differed. `holdTick` is removed; the lift waits for the pause to end and the pause changes nothing (checks above).
- Found 16 Oct 2026, physics not changed: **the restitution slider's 0.1 is 0.02 at the table.** Bullet multiplies the two bodies' restitution (and friction), and the table's is 0.2. The slider's help said only "bounciness"; it now gives the product, and a comment at the table gives both products (friction 0.4, restitution 0.02 at the table; 0.25 and 0.01 body on body). No theory check uses the restitution; the value was chosen, not measured, and a resin body on a wooden table bounces more than 0.02, so the bounce here is on the low side. It is left as it is, since raising it would change the validated statistics.
- **Rolling resistance and spin friction are script-side models.** They are constant moments, not Bullet physics. δ = 150 µm and 0.7 rad/s² were chosen, not measured. The findings PDF (Table 5b) shows that the outcome depends strongly on δ: without rolling resistance nothing ever settles, Gomboc-C included.
- **Gomboc-C stops short of upright, and on the hull up to ~12° at 9 cm.** Investigated 19 Oct 2026 (GC_SEED=1, defaults, 4200 frames, current and 9fe0ecc scripts; GC_LOG plus a probe copy that logs every frame and Bullet's contact points). The two 11°+ rests: Yellow #3 (current script, "at rest after 17.90 s, 11.6 deg") and Purple #5 (both scripts, "6.50 s, 11.4 deg"). Both were on the flat (x = 22.0 cm, z = −11.4 and +13.6; the flat runs to |x| = 69, |z| = 55.5 cm, so no ramp), the nearest other gömböc 11.3 cm away (contact needs < 9.2 cm), tilted toward body −z (the trough), with the centre of mass 3.52762–3.52763 cm above the table: exactly the 11.72° hull face's 3.48762 cm plus the 0.04 cm margin (upright: 3.5252–3.5253). So they lay on that false resting face, or tipped over its downhill edge by α, where gravity's lever is 3.488 cm·α − 62.9 µm, under δ = 150 µm for α < 0.35°: the hold band is 11.38–11.72°. Bullet's own contact points (v:eachContact, touching ones only) confirm it: 2–3 points, all with the table, spread up to ±11 mm along the body's x axis (the trough runs across the long, nearly straight bottom) and all at one offset across it, so the lever is that offset. Yellow #3: +8 µm at 43.3 s growing to +46 µm at 49.3 s (on the face side: gravity turns it toward lying flat). Purple #5: −55 µm at 66.9 s growing to −151 µm at 69.15 s, when it broke away (−373 µm at 69.4 s, 5°/s). Predicted from the tilt alone: 11 µm on the face side for Yellow #3 (measured 8–11), 53 µm off it for Purple #5 (measured 55), 149 µm at the break (measured 151). So the body was held exactly while gravity's lever was under δ, as the rolling-resistance model says. Yellow #3 crept from 11.634° to 11.700° (toward lying flat on the face) over 6 s at under 0.07°/s. Purple #5 crept from 11.53° to 11.40° in 2 s (0.06°/s, under the detector's 0.1°/s) and then rolled off at 11.3–11.4° (predicted edge of the band: 11.38°) to 2.1° (the old script re-reports it at rest at 2.1°; the current run ended first). Causes: (b) the hull's facets, which extend where δ can hold the body from about 7° on the smooth surface out to narrow bands as far as 21°, plus (d) for Purple, declared at rest while creeping off. Not (c) and not a reporting bug: the tilt is measured from body +y, which is the true resting normal (the hull's lowest point lies at (±1.125, −3.48531, 0), a 2.25 cm flat ridge under the centre of mass). The smooth-surface numbers are estimates: only the hull's points (on the true surface) are here, not the CAD surface, and the lever is the derivative of the support height smoothed over 1–3°. The header's "0.3-8 deg ... That is real" is corrected. Possible changes, not applied because they change behaviour or output: a finer tessellation along the trough (the bands narrow with the facet size, but a finer hull costs time and would need its false faces re-checked), or a console note when a rest lies on one of the hull's 34 resting faces. Only comments and documentation changed (the tilt explanation, and the CAD model's credit in gomboc-drop-c.lua, gomboc-variety.lua, gomboc-hull.lua and gomboc.obj's first line, which stays one short line: the variety writes scaled copies of the .obj to temporary files without the .obj ending, and with a longer header bpp failed to load them). Checks: Gomboc Drop C, GC_SEED=1, 4200 frames, console identical to the unedited script; Gomboc Variety standalone (GV_SEED=3 GV_COUNT=4 GV_REDROP=1, 1200 frames) identical to the unedited files; hall against standalone: IDENTICAL (32 run lines); HOLD_S=0 for 900 frames a prefix of the paused run for 930 (31 lines).
- **The Sloan shapes are not strictly convex at these β.** They roll on their convex hull. The findings PDF cites arXiv:2604.17120, which finds strict convexity only below β ≈ 0.036. The hull gains a second basin at higher β. Re-checked 15 Oct 2026 by re-running the reconstruction's basin scan (scanb.py's method, form 1, 25000 surface points) at finer β: deepest extra basin 5.9 µm at 0.08 (the hull facets' own noise is ~5 µm), 11.9 µm at 0.085, 25.3 at 0.09, 43.0 at 0.095, 66.7 at 0.10 (the PDF's Table 5 gives 67 at 0.10 and none at 0.08). The lua comment ("first found at beta 0.085") was consistent with this; it now gives the depths and notes that the PDF rounds the limit to "above β ≈ 0.09". The scan result is recorded in variety-reconstruction/README.txt. The PDF was not edited.
- **The β 0.08 resting point:** the lua says its lowest point is ~35° from the formula's minimum; computed 15 Oct 2026 from the `down` and `top` vectors in gomboc-meshes/beta1-08.lua: 34.6° (β 0.07: 4.6°). The findings PDF §4 says the Sloan shapes' "true resting orientation lies 4–5° from the formula's minimum", which holds for β 0.07 only; its own §5 says the balancing point sits ~35° off the top on β 0.08. This inconsistency inside the PDF is now noted in variety-reconstruction/README.txt (there is no Gomboc README.md). The PDF was not edited.
- **Re-reported rests (creep).** Several bodies were reported "at rest" again and again: Yellow #3 (125%) five times between 19.6 and 32.4 s, and one β 0.07 three times. Each time the body had moved more than 0.2° or 0.2 mm within 2 s after being declared at rest, which matches the contact-solver creep admitted in gomboc-drop-c.lua ("up to ~0.3 deg and ~0.7 mm per minute"). The tally uses the last of these times. Re-measured 18 Oct 2026 for one Gomboc-C (GC_COUNT=1 GC_SEED=1, 108 s after rest): 0.12°/min of rotation and 0.56 mm/min of slide at 1200 steps/s, and 0.07°/min at 4800. That is far below the ~0.85°/s creep another study found for small, slender uniform polyhedra in Bullet; details are in the Bille dossier.
- Fixed on 15 Oct 2026, tally median: with an even number of runs the "median" used to print the lower middle value; it is now the mean of the two middle values, in gomboc-variety.lua and gomboc-drop-c.lua. Seeded re-run: "Gomboc-C at 125%  2 runs, time to rest median 6.4 s (2.6-10.3)" = mean of 2.58 and 10.27 s.
- **Collisions between bodies** (props, knocks) affect individual results. The console flags them as "touching".
- Fixed on 15 Oct 2026: variety-reconstruction/README.txt listed meshes beta1-06 and beta2-04, which are not in gomboc-meshes/. It now names the two kept (beta1-07, beta1-08), says gen.py's __main__ makes a different larger-β set, and that the kept files' `down` is the lowest face of the hull's one basin, not the formula minimum gen.py writes. Sloan's form 2 is left out by design.
- Statistics are from one short run. The Sloan upright fraction (0/16) is compatible with the 9% in the findings PDF, given the small sample.
