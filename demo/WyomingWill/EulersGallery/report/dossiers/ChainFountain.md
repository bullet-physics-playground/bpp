## The Chain Fountain

**What it shows** — A ball chain (4.5 mm balls 6.5 mm apart, as Biggins and Warner used), poured into a jar 1.5 m above the floor as a jumbled heap, is drawn over the rim by an unseen hand and let go. It then siphons out on its own, and rises in an arch above the rim instead of sliding over it. This is the "Mould effect", filmed by Steve Mould in 2013 and explained by Biggins and Warner in 2014. A link picked up by one end rotates and pushes its other end into the pile, and the pile's upward reaction throws the chain up. A rope, with no links, would just slide over. Since 16 Oct 2026 the default is this poured heap; H (or CF_PILE=coil) gives the coil of 1.5 cm links the exhibit had before, kept as the comparison.

**Model**
- Default (since 16 Oct 2026): the poured heap. Chain: 1500 Bullet capsule links (9.75 m), 0.65 cm joint to joint, radius 0.225 cm, the mass at the ends (I = m L^2/4 + 0.4 m r^2, as balls on light rods; CF_BALLI), cone-twist joints with a 30 deg swing limit, true friction 0.3 (0.548 a body; Bullet multiplies), no restitution, no damping. Since 20 Oct 2026 the links' gyroscopic term is solved with the joints and contacts by bpp (body.gyroSolved; CF_GYRO=solver, the heap's default; Bullet's own term never acts on bodies this light). Drawn with chain-fountain-meshes/bead-link.obj.
- Heap: poured once offline (CF_POUR=1 CF_SEED=2 CF_SAVEHEAP; every link live; a hand 6 cm over the jar floor wandering within 2 cm of the axis, 1 m/s), settled, stored as joint points from the jar floor up (chain-fountain-meshes/heap.txt). In the exhibit its links are fixed bodies except a live zone: every link within 4 cm across of the pick-up and no more than 1.5 cm below it (CF_ZONE, CF_ZDEPTH; 0.5 cm until 20 Oct 2026), plus the 10 links after the pick-up; live heap links 1 cm outside the zone that moved under 0.1 mm in the last 1/100 s are fixed again (until 20 Oct 2026: every 18 steps, by their speed then); a live link next to a fixed one is held to it by a cone-twist joint. Each link keeps one fixed body, parked below the floor while it moves (hall identity).
- Run: a scripted hand draws the end up 4 cm over the rim, out to x = 12 cm, down at 1.2 g to the floor, and lets go (0.64 s). 1800 steps/s (slider min 1800 in heap mode), 20 iterations, joint ERP 0.5 (Bullet's 0.2 is not step-converged), touching contacts only (CPT 0). Starts at 1/8 speed (Z: 1/8 <-> 1).
- Sliders in heap mode: bending held at 30 deg (the heap was poured so; moving it puts it back and the console says so once); layers inert; friction restarts; height works.
- Both piles: links below y = 2.5 cm are frozen (joint removed, drawn only), so b = 0. Wake, freeze and measurement run after every physics step; output is the same at any display speed.
- The coil (H, CF_PILE=coil), as before:
- Chain: Bullet rigid capsule links, 1.5 cm joint-to-joint, radius 0.3 cm, mass 0.5 g, no damping, no restitution. Neighbours are joined by btConeTwistConstraint with a swing limit equal to the bending angle (default 30 deg; CF_BEND; slider 20-90 deg, was 10-90) and free twist. Friction 0.3 set on links, glass and disc; Bullet multiplies the two bodies' values, so every chain contact has 0.09 (found 16 Oct 2026; with a true 0.3, CF_MU=0.548, the fountain is unchanged: 8.6-9.8 cm at 30 deg, 5.1-5.7 at 90).
- Pile and jar: a rigid disc stands in for the coil, lowered one layer (0.65 cm) when the first link of a new layer is woken. The jar is 24 glass slabs (7 cm inside radius, 8 cm wall).
- Initial coil (changed 15 Oct 2026): flat spirals, outside in then inside out, all turning the same way. Every link is 1.5 cm and no joint is laid bent past its limit. The inner radius is max(3 cm, the tightest the chain can turn while tipping down) = 3.1 cm at 30 deg, 4.8 cm at 20. At a layer's end the chain tips down 0.65 cm over 3 links, going on round. 10 layers: 15.8 m (1053 links) at 30 deg, 9.6 m at 20, 16.1 m at 60/90. Waiting links are drawn, not simulated.
- Simulation window: links are woken so that 10 links ahead of the pick-up still lie where they were laid, at most 1200 per simulated second. The last link woken is held at its far end by a cone-twist joint to a fixed body in the next link's place. Links below y = 2.5 cm are frozen: their joint is removed and they become drawn-only, non-colliding meshes. About 130-200 links are active.
- Stepping: exactly 1/1800 s at every display speed. Each frame takes as many whole steps as its share of simulated time. Wake, freeze and measurement run after every step. 20 solver iterations.
- Bullet does all dynamics and contacts. The script does wake, anchor, freeze, the pile disc, and (read-only) measurement.

**Theory checks** — Biggins and Warner (2014): pile push a m v^2 up on the link being lifted, floor pull b m v^2. Steady flow gives v^2 = g h1/(1 - a - b) and h2 = a v^2/g (narrow arch). Here b = 0 by construction (frozen links, no joint, no collisions). Fresh runs, 10 layers, CF_SPEED=1, steady window t = 1.00 s to the last coil link woken. v = rate links land x 1.5 cm. "Push" = disc contact force minus the weight of links touching it, divided by m v^2 (CF_PUSH=1). "Carried" (new, CF_PUSH=1) = -(T - m v_i^2 - m g y)/(m v^2) on the freely falling chain 20-100 cm below the pick-up. T is the tension worked out from the links' accelerations, summed up from the free lowest link. A steady B&W chain keeps this equal to a from pile to floor.

**Known departures and honest caveats**
- Fixed 15 Oct 2026: frame-rate dependence (all work per physics step, fixed 1/1800 s) and the 60-degree stall (last woken link anchored; landed links don't collide).
- Fixed 15 Oct 2026: the coil was laid wrong. The spiral went in to 3 cm at every bending, so at 20 deg 304 of 1054 joints were laid bent past their stop (worst about 29 deg against 20). At every bending each layer began with a 0.65 cm upright link (90 deg bends) and the next layer wound the other way. When woken, the solver kicked these joints straight. The old "stiffer chain, higher fountain" (20 deg median 11.1 cm) came from those kicks: with the fixed coil it is 4.7. Only the lead's 3 corners, woken at t = 0, are still laid past the limit.
- Measurement changes (15 Oct): v is now the rate links land, not the rate they are woken. At 20 deg and 4800 steps/s the waking ran ahead (wake rate 592 cm/s, above the sqrt(2 g h1) = 542 energy bound). New lines: landing speed and energy, and with CF_PUSH=1 the directly measured push. CF_PUSH is off by default because eachContact makes the run 2.6x slower in the Gallery (34.7 s against 13.2 s for 1200 frames).
- **Located 15 Oct 2026, mechanism still open: the fountain is low because the pile's push doesn't reach the moving chain.** The pile pushes up with a = 0.13-0.31, but the falling chain carries only a third or less of it. A pile whose pick-up stays put (flake on a table) raises the fountain only a little (ratio 0.36-0.40 against 0.29-0.30 for the spiral on the same table). No model channel tried (step x2 and x4, iterations x2.5 and x5, friction 0, joint stops off, link size x0.5, look-ahead 5/15) brings the ratio above 0.50. With the push the chain does carry, the flake obeys all three B&W relations: height within 21%, speed within 4%, energy within 4 points. So the earlier apparent 11-30% energy loss mostly came from using the contact push as a. The gap is in the pick-up zone. The chain is dragged and lifted over several links (still only about 90% of full speed 4-6 cm above the pile, from the inferred tension), not picked up a link at a time. This does not change with step or iterations, so it is not solver convergence. Without joint stops the push itself halves. On the spiral, and with half-size links, the falling chain carries 0.00-0.04 while the fountain stands higher than that would give. So between pile and falling leg those flows are not steady either. Whether real chains also carry only part of the contact push is unknown; B&W's own h2 = 0.14 h1 means a = 0.12 against their rod model's 1/6.
- Not step-converged at 20 deg (1/3600 s: v +6%, h2 +85%). The run is chaotic, with spreads as in the table.
- The waking scheme is not neutral. With 5 links woken ahead instead of 10 (scratch copy with CF_K), v = 476 / 452 cm/s at 30 / 60 deg (+20% / +6%) and a from speed 0.35 / 0.28, against a push of 0.21 / 0.16. The anchor's force is not measured. With 20 the woken links are dragged into a heap (276-446 links moving; at 60 deg the rim carries 1.3 m v^2). So v is known to about 20%.
- **Tested 15 Oct 2026: the wake/anchor shortcut does not lose the push, but it does make the fountain too tall.** With every link simulated, the moving chain still carries none of the pile's push (carried -0.13 to -0.19), so the lost push is how rigid links come off a pile in this model. The full chain drags the top of the pile and rubs the rim (0.26-0.32 m v^2). Its fountain is 8.6 / 6.3 / 5.5 cm against the exhibit's 10.6 / 10.8 / 11.2 at 30 / 60 / 90 deg. The exhibit's fountain is therefore up to 2x taller than the full simulation of the same chain. A cheaper scheme (4 live top layers, no anchor) matches the full chain at 30 deg, but costs 21 ms/frame at quarter speed against 4.4 now (full: 35 ms; budget 16.7). The default is unchanged; the README says so. The flake pile could not be run all-live: on the bare table the live 25-layer stack is dragged off as a mass.

- **Changed 20 Oct 2026 (exhibit physics unchanged; option and proposed bpp change added): the links' gyroscopic term.** Verified: Bullet 3.24's term (flag BT_ENABLE_GYROSCOPIC_FORCE_IMPLICIT_BODY, on for every body; bpp never changes it) is computed by btRigidBody::computeGyroscopicImpulseImplicit_Body and solved with btMatrix3x3::solve33, which divides by the determinant only above FLT_EPSILON = 1.19e-7 and otherwise multiplies by it; a heap link's (I = 4.39e-6, 2.73e-5, 2.73e-5 kg cm^2) is 3.3e-15, so the links get no term (a free link spinning 1000 + 600 rad/s moves the same with the flag on or off; its L turns 82 deg in 1 s). Masses x 1024 with the term off reproduce the run byte for byte, and with it on bring in Bullet's formula, which is backward Euler: a free spinning link loses 69% of its energy in 0.5 s, its turning across wiped out. Significance: the links spin about their axes at 150-220 rad/s rms, and leaving the term out makes that spin turn as if it had the inertia across the link (6.2x): 13-32% of the links' angular accelerations per step; the true gyroscopic torque is about 5% at the pick-up and 0.2% in the arch. Implementations: on a free link the exact rotation for a symmetric body keeps E and |L| to 1e-5 (L direction within 5 deg over 2 s), the implicit midpoint likewise (phase error, 24 deg); forward Euler blows up. **But every form added before the step (the previous audit's way, also implicit midpoint, Bullet's formula, Adams-Bashforth, a gyrostat form) makes a jointed chain unstable**: a hanging chain of spinning links (60 links, 300 rad/s, no contacts) gains energy without end, e-folding in 0.1 s at 1800 steps/s, 0.3 s at 7200, 1.2 s at 28800 (an O(dt) instability; without the term it stays still), independent of ERP, iterations, joint type; in the fountain the falling chain's turning across was 273 rad/s against 43. The previous audit's 0.164-0.166 was this instability. A Python copy of Bullet's step shows the cure: the term inside the solve, the body answering impulses through (I + dt/2 Jg)^-1 (linearly implicit midpoint). As a bpp change (demo/WyomingWill/EulersGallery/exhibits/ChainFountain/reconstruction/bpp-gyro-midpoint.diff, src/viewer.cpp only, built and tested in a scratch copy, not merged): free bodies keep E to 1e-5 (asymmetric: 8e-5, L within 0.7 deg over 2 s); the hanging chain stays still at 1800 and 7200, 300 and 1000 rad/s; output with no body marked is byte-identical to stock bpp. Heap study with it (the five heaps of section 12, every link simulated): h2/h1 clear of the rim 0.132 / 0.133 / 0.131 at 1800 / 3600 / 7200 steps/s against 0.140 / 0.137 / 0.140 without (paired shift -0.007 +- 0.008; v 424-455 cm/s either way, 0.78-0.84 sqrt(2 g h1)); runs caught on the rim 6 of 30 with the term, 2 of 30 without (all-run means 0.109-0.131 against 0.126-0.140).
