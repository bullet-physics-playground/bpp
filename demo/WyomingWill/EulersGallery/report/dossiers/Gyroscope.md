## The Gyroscope

**What it shows** — Three identical toy gyroscopes spun to 100 turns/s. Left: held only by one end of its axle on a pedestal point, it does not fall but precesses with the axle level. Middle: the same, hung by a string. Right: axle upright, standing on a tightrope on a forked foot, balancing like a top on its point; an attendant rocks the wire 1 degree each way so it slides to and fro, upright, about 9 cm each way. As the rotors slow, the left two precess faster and sag; the tightrope one wobbles wider and finally comes off.

**Model**
- Gyroscope: one Bullet rigid body (hoop 50 g, axle 10 g, rotor 100 g; M = 160 g). Rotor r = 3 cm, I_rotor = 0.65 m r^2 = 0.585 kg cm^2; body moments (code): axial 1.069, in-hoop-plane 0.849, across-hoop 1.333 kg cm^2. Holding balls at r = 6.0 cm from the centre; the foot's wire contact 6.38 cm (PIV + ball + wire radius).
- Rotor spin is a scalar angular momentum h = I_rotor*S inside the body (gyrostat); Bullet never spins a body at 100 turns/s. Bullet's gyroscopic term is off; the script applies -w x (I w + h) each step, 2nd-order Adams-Bashforth (README: a one-step torque grows the wobble; a direct velocity update makes it sag steadily), as the angular impulse C*dt just before the step (applyTorqueImpulse; until 20 Oct 2026 as a torque with applyTorque, which Bullet 3.24's friction rows leave out: see the caveats).
- Pedestal: btPoint2PointConstraint at the ball's centre; released when the axle sags 40 deg below level. String: a 2 g, 12 cm rigid rod with ball joints at both ends. Wire: kinematic capsule, radius 0.06 cm, 30 cm long; foot = sphere + two capsule prongs; foot-wire friction 0.0025 at the contact: the friction slider (GY_MU, 0.005) sets the wire's, the frame keeps Bullet's default 0.5, and Bullet multiplies the two. It slides on slopes > 0.14 deg (still wire: holds at 0.1 deg, 0.4 cm in 8 s; slides at 0.2 deg, 13 cm in 8 s; checked 16 Oct 2026; 20 Oct: 0.5 cm and 13.6 cm, the 0.5 the wobble's sway and a slow creep). Frame on table or posts: 0.5 × 0.6 = 0.3. Restitution 0 everywhere.
- Losses: rotor bearing 1 turn/s per second; pivot friction 0.5e-3 N m against any turning at the pedestal (a quarter at the string's knot). Rocking: wire tipped +/-1 deg about its middle in 0.6 s (cosine ramp), always up on the gyroscope's side, judged 0.3 s ahead, with timing adapted to reach ~9 cm each way.
- 2400 physics steps/s, 30 solver iterations, no damping, g = 981 cm/s^2.

**Known departures and honest caveats**
- The rotor is not simulated as a spinning body; the gyroscopic torque is applied explicitly (Adams-Bashforth). The README says the more obvious alternatives either grow the wobble or make it sag steadily.
- Sagging comes from a chosen pivot-friction torque (0.5e-3 N m), not from a contact model; the 40 deg release from the pedestal is a scripted rule; the string is a rigid 2 g rod, not a flexible string.
- The foot friction ("as if it ran on a little wheel") is chosen so the gyroscope slides on a 1 deg tilt. It is 0.0025 at the contact, not the 0.005 the slider shows (see the next point). The attendant is a feedback controller (lead 0.3 s, adaptive timing), not physics.
- Fixed 15 Oct 2026: the precession rate in README and lua header (was 2.67 rad/s, 2.35 s per turn, at 100 t/s; that is the value at about 96 t/s) now reads 2.56 rad/s, 2.45 s, from the stated m = 160 g, r = 6 cm, I = 0.65 m r^2; the "3.4 turns in 8 s" now says 3.35 (theory 3.40, console rounds to 3.4).
- Fixed 15 Oct 2026: rocked 1.5 deg, README and header now give the measured 49.0 s (51 t/s), shorter than the still wire's 55.0 s; only 1 deg (62.0 s) outlasts the still wire. The "about 40"/"about 50" turns/s wording is replaced by the run values: rough still wire 52, slippery still wire 45, rocked 1 deg 38; pedestal off at 37 s.
- Was open (15-17 Oct 2026; resolved 20 Oct, below): rocked 1.5 deg the attendant does not hold 9 cm. Its turn points scatter from 1.2 to 14.7 cm (at 1 deg: 4.8-12.5 after a first 13.9). The attendant's correction is an integral on the reach error (gain 0.3, offset clamped at +/-4 cm); at 1.5 deg the same timing error gives 1.5x the distance, and in the last 6 s before it falls it overshoots to 14-15 cm with the offset at its clamp. The wobble is wider (9-17 deg from upright vs 2-8 at 1 deg, 17-28 deg in the last swings); the README says that most likely the wobble pushes the foot along too, and that this has not been separated from the slope's push. Nothing in the physics was changed.
- The sleeping-top threshold is only approximate here: the foot is a sphere sliding on a thin capsule, not a fixed point; the README itself explains the slippery foot shifts the relevant I toward the centre of mass.
- Found 16 Oct 2026, documented, physics not changed: **the foot-wire friction was half what was stated.** Bullet multiplies the two bodies' friction; the slider (0.005) is set on the wire, the frame was left at Bullet's 0.5, so the foot has 0.0025 (and "rough", GY_MU=0.3, is 0.15). The README and lua said 0.005, sliding on slopes over 0.3 deg; it slides over 0.14 deg (checked above). Making the stated 0.005 true (frame 1.0, table and posts 0.3 so that the frame on the table stays 0.3) changed the validated tightrope results a lot: rocked 1 deg off at 53.9 s, 46 t/s (was 62.0 s, 38 t/s); still slippery wire 53.6 s, 46 t/s (55.0, 45); still rough (true 0.3) 43.0 s, 57 t/s (48.2, 52); rocked 1.5 deg still up at 60 s (49.0); rocked 2 deg 18.2 s (12.7); rocked 3 deg still up at 10 s (5.9). With a true 0.005, rocking 1 deg no longer outlasts the still wire. Since 0.005 was a chosen number, not a measured one, the physics is kept and the README, lua comments, slider and help text now give the values the foot really has (0.0025; 0.15). The pedestal and string gyroscopes do not touch anything and are unchanged (pedestal off at 37.2 s in every run).
- Step size (20 Oct 2026): the tightrope's results at 1200 / 2400 / 4800 steps/s are in the table (rough still wire 47/47/48 t/s to wobble; rocked 1 deg 60.0/60.8/60.4 s; 2 deg 8.4/8.5/9.4 s; 3 deg 5.9/5.9/6.0 s; still 57.0/57.8/58.5 s). Rocked 1.5 deg is not converged and can't be: 46.6/62.9/64.0 s (see below). The pedestal and string runs remain at 2400 only.
- **Changed 20 Oct 2026: the gyroscopic torque is an angular impulse, not applyTorque.** Bullet 3.24's friction rows (btSequentialImpulseConstraintSolver::setupFrictionConstraint) include a body's external force but not its external torque, while its normal rows, joint rows and final velocity include both (found on the gömböc, 20 Oct; see Gomboc). A body held by friction under a steady torque C therefore slides at h*C*dt/I. The tightrope's foot, held by friction against the gyroscopic torque, did: on the still, rough wire (GY_MU=0.3) its contact point slid 0.044 cm/s at 2-10 s (0.84 deg from upright) where h*C*dt/I, from the logged torque, gives 0.031-0.048 (I across the axle 1.33-0.85 kg cm^2), and 0.065 / 0.106 / 0.30 cm/s (predicted 0.045-0.071, 0.072-0.112, 0.22-0.35) as the wobble grew; the top started to wobble at 66 t/s, step-dependent (69 / 66 / 61 at 1200 / 2400 / 4800 steps/s). As applyTorqueImpulse(C*dt) before the step the foot's slip is 0.010-0.031 cm/s up to 40 s, a third or less of what the missed torque would give (what is left is likely Bullet's single friction direction; rolling.anchor, tried, moved the result by 1 t/s and was not adopted), and it wobbles at 47 t/s at every step: the sleeping-top rule's 46.7-48.2. The pedestal and the string are not changed by this (Bullet's joint rows have the torque; tilt the same to 0.01 deg over 35 s). On the slippery wire the foot slides along the wire nearly all the time (median 7 cm/s against an h*C*dt/I of 0.13 cm/s; friction saturated at mu*N), so there the change is small, but it moves the tightrope's times (rocked 1 deg 62.0 -> 60.8 s; still 55.0 -> 57.8; 2 deg 12.7 -> 8.5; 1.5 deg 49.0 -> 62.9). README, lua header and the in-code comment now give the new values. Hall: cmp2.sh gyro, 1800 frames: IDENTICAL (15 run lines); HOLD_S=0 1800 frames is a prefix of the default 1830 (19 lines).
- **Resolved 20 Oct 2026: the 14-15 cm turn points rocked 1.5 deg are the posts at the wire's ends, and the cause is not the friction artefact.** The wire is 30 cm long; a post stands 0.5 cm past each end; the foot's ball (radius 0.32) meets the post at 14.68 cm, and the turn points piled up at 14.5-14.7 cm (13.9-14.1 with the body tilted). Logging the tightrope's contacts showed it against the post (contact normal along the wire) at exactly those times (2.6, 4.4 and 42-47 s). Along the wire, the only forces on the body are the slope and the foot's friction (every contact normal with the wire is square to it); from each crossing of the middle a point mass on the logged wire tilt with Coulomb friction 0.0025 turns where the centre of mass turned (1 deg: rms 0.085 cm over 28 half-swings; 1.5 deg: within 0.3 cm except where a post stopped it), so the wobble does not push it along. Run in Python with the same attendant, a point mass rocked 1.5 deg goes 16.0 cm on its first slide (past the post) and then settles at 9 cm (offset -2.7 to +1.9 cm); rocked 1 deg it gives 13.4 first (the gyroscope: 13.95), then 9. In the gyroscope the attendant watches the foot, which a 20-28 deg wobble puts up to 3 cm off the centre of mass; with such a wobble its timing fails and the swings grow to the posts. Whether the wobble builds up rocked 1.5 deg is chaotic: of six runs (applyTorque and impulse, 1200/2400/4800 steps/s) it built up (12-26 deg, posts at 42-47 s, off at 47-49 s) in two, applyTorque at 2400 and impulse at 1200, and stayed at 2-12 deg (off at 62-64 s, or still up at 65 s) in four. So the fix did not cure it: at 2400 it now happens to take the quiet branch. The "9 cm" is the attendant's set point (GY_REACH), not a physical prediction; the physics check is the along-wire replay above.
- Fixed 17 Oct 2026: **the start pause changed the console.** The `t=` lines every 2 s were printed on bpp's frame count (`N % 120`), which counts the pause's frames too, so with the pause they came at t = 1.5, 3.5, ... s instead of 2.0, 4.0, .... gyroscope.lua now counts only the frames it runs (the same for the log file). With HOLD_S=0 the output is byte-identical to before (1800 frames); HOLD_S=0 for 1800 frames is now a prefix of the default for 1830 (also at 900 and 2400); hall against standalone (cmp2.sh, 1800 frames): IDENTICAL (15 run lines).
