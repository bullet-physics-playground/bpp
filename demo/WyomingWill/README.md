## Demos

*   `linkage.lua`: A viewer for the 10 walking linkages compared in the linkage_c reference tool (Chebyshev-Spears, Hoeckens-Spears, Chebyshev Lambda, Original Hoecken Slider, True 4-Bar Hoekens, Spears 4Bar-1, Jansen, Klann, TrotBot, Strider), driven by the same analytic forward kinematics and duty/flatness/mono metrics, ported straight from that tool's C source rather than simulated with Bullet rigid bodies. Press **F1**/**F2** to step to the previous/next mechanism; each one is normalized to a consistent on-screen size with its full-cycle foot path traced as a static curve. Mechanism name, link/slider/twin counts, and metrics print to the console on every switch.
*   `uniform-coverage2.lua`: A port of the Android app "Uniform Coverage", which asks how evenly a swarm of wandering agents covers an arena. Each agent walks in a straight line for up to a mean-free-path number of steps before picking a new random heading, and also turns early when it is about to hit the boundary or another agent is close ahead -- an idea borrowed from the mean free path of a gas molecule. The arena floor is a 3x3 grid of colored tiles (corner blue, edge green, center red) and the agents are white spheres riding above it; nine poles off to the side form a live histogram whose markers climb to each cell's share of the total visits, with a yellow ring marking the ideal 1/9. Press **S** to start/stop, **R** to re-scatter the agents, **]**/**[** to add/remove five agents, **,**/**.** to slow down/speed up, and **=**/**-** to change how many algorithm ticks run per rendered frame; agent count, mean free path, delay and ticks-per-frame are also GUI sliders. Tick count, agent count, the coverage deviation from perfect uniformity and the theoretical mean free path print to the console about once a second.

## Clocks

Weight-driven clocks simulated as real rigid bodies: a gear train, an escapement, a swinging balance, second/minute/hour hands, and tick/tock sounds. Each one self-tunes toward the real current time and prints a `[hand check]` line about once a second that decodes each hand's actual current angle back into the time it indicates, next to the OS and monotonic clocks.

*   `Clocks/N7_Clock_handcheck.lua`: The "Palette and Pendulum" movement -- a pendulum swinging about the X axis through a full gear train, with the second hand welded straight to the escape wheel and the minute and hour hands driven 60:1 and 720:1 down from it. Gravity is the primary speed lever (the beat period scales as 1/sqrt(gravity), so 3x faster needs roughly 9x the value, not 3x) and is a live GUI slider; a stopwatch across 42-beat blocks compares simulated time against real time and trims gravity to keep them in step. Press **T** to start that training, **G** to lock gravity where it is, **S** to mute the beat sound.
*   `Clocks/N2_Clock_handcheck.lua`: The same clock mirrored across X, so the escapement runs counter-clockwise and the gear train comes back the other way. Everything else -- the block-stopwatch tuning, the recoil filter, the hands, the sound -- is as in N7.
*   `Clocks/E3_Clock_handcheck.lua`: The same movement with the pendulum swapped for a classic verge escapement: a foliot rotating about the vertical axis, with no gravity restoring force at all, so the traditional regulation lever applies instead -- a live `foliotWeight` slider that slides equal weights along the foliot bar, changing its moment of inertia to speed the beat up or slow it down. Its calibration holds the weight fixed and averages the real beat period, swapping the anchor and re-averaging when the error is large. Keys and stopwatch tuning as above.
*   `Clocks/sound/`: `tick.wav` and `tock.wav`, extracted from a clock-ticking recording, played on each beat. Needs bpp built with SDL_mixer.

## Pinball

A playable pinball table in the style of Williams' 1983 *Time Fantasy*, with Bullet physics for the ball, flippers, pop bumpers and slingshots, an editable rules engine, a seven-segment backbox scoreboard, a layout editor for reshaping the playfield, and a computer player that learns as it plays (Q-learning on the flippers, a multi-armed bandit on the plunger). See `Pinball/README.md` for the files, the keys and the default rules.

*   `Pinball/pinball-machine-a.lua`: The table -- open this one.
*   `Pinball/pinball-machine-a-rules.lua`: Points, target banks, bonus, multiplier, extra balls, balls per game, and which sound plays when. Edit it and reload.
*   `Pinball/pinball-machine-a-ai.lua`: The computer player, bound to **P**.
*   `Pinball/pinball-machine-a-brain.lua`: What it has learned so far (the one included has played about a thousand games). Delete it to start over.
*   `Pinball/pinball-machine-a-train.lua`: Trains it headless and much faster than real time: `bpp -f pinball-machine-a-train.lua -n 1000000` from this folder.
*   `Pinball/pinball_machine_sounds/`: Sound effects. Its README lists the expected names; missing ones are skipped.

## Pool

A one-player game of pool on a 9-foot table: fifteen numbered balls, a keyboard-driven cue, and a scoreboard, with Bullet doing the break, the cushions, the pockets and the cue ball's spin (follow, draw and side). Aiming guide, three camera positions, ball in hand, and a best-score save. See `Pool/README.md` for the keys and the physics notes.

*   `Pool/pool-table.lua`: The table -- open this one.
*   `Pool/pool-table-meshes/`: The cue and the ball markings (number spots, stripes, the cue ball's dots).
*   `Pool/pool-table-sounds/`: Sound effects. Its README lists the names; replace any with your own.

Both the pinball and the pool demos need bpp with the keyboard hook (`v:onKey`), the `v:playSound(id, volume)` overload and the `collides` object property -- the changes to `src/viewer.cpp`, `src/viewer.h`, `src/objects/object.cpp` and `src/objects/object.h` that come with them -- plus SDL_mixer for the sound.

## Walkers

A family of walking machines, all built the same way: real Bullet rigid bodies and hinge constraints driven by hinge motors, not analytic forward kinematics, so the solver has to make the gait work. Every one of them carries the same GUI sliders (`maxSubSteps`, `Speed` and `cube_d`, `cubeMass`, `terrainAmp`, `linkageSpacing`, with changes to the dimensions tearing down and rebuilding the whole scene) and runs over an uneven terrain mesh. The ground link lengths are transcribed from the `linkage_c` reference tool's `mechanisms.c`, the same source `linkage.lua` is ported from.

The four-bar walkers are all descended from Chebyshev's Plantigrade Machine of the 1878 Paris World Exhibition: four copies of a crank-rocker four-bar on a cube body, pendants carrying wide flat feet, and crossbars bracing pairs of legs. The cranks are driven by hinge motors rather than by setting velocities from a `preSim` hook, because the physics runs in up to 20 substeps per `timeStep` and a motor is a bias the solver applies every one of them.

*   `Walkers/Chebyshev_normalT.lua`: The base machine -- four Chebyshev Lambda four-bars, two on the front face and two mirrored on the back, the back pair running 90 degrees out of phase with the front. Each front/back pair is joined by two crossbars, one above and one below.
*   `Walkers/Chebyshev_diagT.lua`: The same, but with the legs braced by diagonal crossbars instead (each front-to-back diagonal pair welded together, a weld that is only kinematically valid between legs sharing a phase), which is what lets it hold its balance.
*   `Walkers/Chebyshev_Spears_normalT.lua`: `Chebyshev_normalT.lua` with the Chebyshev Lambda linkage swapped for Chebyshev-Spears (crank 36, ground 48, coupler = rocker = leg = 110, ground angle 45 degrees, from `linkage.lua`'s `compute_chebyshev_spears`, scaled so the ground link stays at 2.5). The coupler is bent 90 degrees at its midpoint and built as one fused rigid body with the bend encoded as a fixed pivot offset, plus a render-only second segment to draw the bend -- an earlier two-body version, welded at the same point as the coupler/rocker hinge, was over-constrained and visibly jittered under motor torque. The whole assembly is flipped so the legs point up above the cube, and the two crossbars per pair were reduced to one taller bar (the pair was over-constrained too).
*   `Walkers/Chebyshev_Spears_diagT.lua`: The original four-legged Chebyshev-Spears walker with the diagonal bracing, kept as MODEL C: exactly one change from the original -- a Z-target mismatch fix at hingeB, and nothing else -- so the one fix can be judged in isolation from the structural changes in the version above.
*   `Walkers/Hoecken_normalT.lua`: The same four-legged machine driven instead by the classical Hoecken straight-line four-bar (the 1.1 : 2.3 : 2.8 : 2.8 : 5.65 proportions from `linkage.lua`'s `compute_hoecken`). Still a closed four-bar loop, but its free coupler point traces a much longer, flatter path.
*   `Walkers/Hoecken_sliderT.lua`: The original 1867 Hoecken slider, the "a, 2a, 10a" variant -- a genuinely different topology rather than different proportions. A crank drives a long rigid bar threaded through a fixed pivot that only constrains the bar to pass through that point (a small free-spinning block hinged to the cube, slider-jointed to the bar, replaces the rocker entirely), and the far end of the bar traces an approximate straight line over a long stroke. The foot therefore makes a far bigger, more nearly linear excursion than in any of the four-bar versions above.
*   `Walkers/Hoecken_Spears_sliderT.lua`: The same slider mechanism with Bill Spears' own proportions (crank 36, ground 46.95, bar 168.98, from `linkage.lua`'s `compute_hoeckens_spears`) instead of Hoecken's classical ones -- crank : ground : bar of 1 : 1.30417 : 4.69389 rather than 1 : 2 : 10.
*   `Walkers/Spears_4Bar_4LegT.lua`: Spears 4Bar-1, a true closed four-bar again (crank 36, ground 223.659 at 54.459 degrees, rocker 121.37, coupler 138.789), but with the leg rigidly welded to the coupler a quarter of the way along it at a fixed angle rather than hanging as a free-hinged pendant -- so there are no crossbars at all. Four legs, each at its own independently optimized phase (0, 225, 180, 225) instead of the front-pair-together pattern the earlier files use, and renormalized to the same 8.323638-unit stride baseline the rest of the series uses.
*   `Walkers/Spears_4Bar_2LegT.lua`: The same walker reduced to two legs on a single mount column (front at phase 0, back at 180). The honest finding, recorded in the file: this configuration has a real stability problem the four-legged version never showed. With no left-right pair there is no lateral base of support, so the asymmetric loading of the two alternating legs produces a net torque nothing counteracts, and the cube's Z rotation grows continuously -- about 62 degrees over 400 frames -- rather than oscillating around a stable value, with net progress then partly reversing. Bullet's angular damping is already clamped at its ceiling, so foot size is the best lever found so far, not a fix. Speed and `maxSubSteps` were both re-tuned against it, which fixed terrain-amplitude robustness (the worst amplitude went from giving back essentially all of its progress to reaching 449 units by frame 1600) but not the underlying tip.
*   `Walkers/Spears_4Bar_2Leg_BalancedT.lua`: The two-legged version with an active balance controller added on top -- a mechanical counterweight hanging straight down from the cube's center, with `barLen` and `tipMass` sliders that rebuild the scene, plus a rebuilt shared axle that removes the startup snap the original shaft produced. It also fixes the axle's construction flaw, keeps the solver at 40 iterations (measured: at Bullet's default 10 the tilt range roughly doubles), applies constraint-force-mixing to the leg hinges so the closed loop is not perfectly rigid, and is tuned for the 2.0-3.0 terrain-amplitude range rather than flat ground.
*   `Walkers/Jansen_6LegT.lua`: Six Theo Jansen "Strandbeest" legs on a shared cube body, using Jansen's own published "holy numbers" for the ground link lengths, taken from the same crossing-free branch choice as `linkage.lua` (an earlier, more commonly cited branch has two links overlapping for 100% of the cycle, which is not physically buildable with real hinges). Each leg is seven rigid bodies rather than eight or eleven, because two of the eleven point-to-point spans in the dyad chain are rigid triangles (G-J2-J3 and J4-F-J5) and are each built as a single fused body -- a choice that also fixed a real measured chirality-flip bug originally found on Klann's much narrower triangle. A red marker trail records the trajectory.
*   `Walkers/Klann_6LegT.lua`: The same six-leg rig with the linkage replaced by Klann's (US Patent 6,260,862), from `linkage.lua`'s `compute_klann` and uniformly scaled by 70 so its native 1-2-unit dimensions fit this file's cube and terrain -- the scale chosen by matching the leg's own full-cycle bounding box to Jansen's ~122x125-unit footprint, which meant `ROW_SPACING`, `cube_d` and the terrain grid resolution did not need re-tuning. Verified by direct computation that all four circle intersections stay well clear of the locked-configuration case across a full crank rotation. A red marker trail records the trajectory.

## Euler's Cabinet of Curiosities

An L-shaped 18th-century walnut gallery of rigid-body curiosities. Only the exhibit you stand at runs; the others wait and carry on when you come back. Tab walks between them. See `Cabinet/README.md`.

*   `Cabinet/cabinet.lua`: The gallery -- open this one. Each exhibit below also runs on its own.
*   `Gomboc/gomboc-drop-c.lua`, `Gomboc/gomboc-variety.lua`: Gombocs (and the Sloan beta shapes) dropped until they right themselves.
*   `Bille/bille-drop.lua`: Bille, the weighted monostable tetrahedron, and the 21-, 26- and 37-corner spiral polyhedra.
*   `Dzhanibekov/dzhanibekov.lua`: The Dzhanibekov (tennis-racket) effect: three T-handles spinning in zero gravity.
*   `Rattleback/rattleback.lua`: Rattlebacks that reverse their spin.
*   `TippeTop/tippe-top.lua`: Tippe tops that turn over onto their stems. See `TippeTop/README.md`.
*   `ChainFountain/chain-fountain.lua`: A bead chain leaping out of a jar in an arch (the Mould effect). See `ChainFountain/README.md`.
